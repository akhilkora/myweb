Designing a Coaxial Swerve Drivetrain

A close look at the coaxial swerve pod I designed for FTC Team #24108 Boltic — belt-driven steering, bevel-gear drive, and a compact two-plate frame.

Each pod of the drivetrain is a steerable drive module that lets a single wheel rotate in any direction while driving, achieving holonomic motion. The assembly packages the hardware between two plates to stay compact and rigid, with steering handled by a belt-driven pulley system and drive routed through bevel gears and pulleys down to the wheel.

Frame & structure

The pod is built on a sandwiched two-plate aluminum frame joined by standoffs. Stacking the plates on standoffs sets a gap for the steering and drive components, and gives the module stiffness without adding a heavy enclosure around it. All mounting uses the goBILDA hex hole/bore pattern, which keeps the plate layout, pulley mounting, and motor placement on a consistent grid.

Steering

The steering runs on an 81-tooth custom-designed pulley that forms the rotation ring. A SWYFT motor drives this ring through a 5 mm HTD timing belt. The HTD belt was chosen over a geared ring for lower friction, quieter steering, and simpler tensioning — keeping the belt under tension is what limits backlash.

Drive

Drive power comes from a separate drive motor and is transmitted down through a pulley system and bevel-gear set to the wheel. Routing the drive along the rotation axis keeps the drive path separate from steering, so rotating the module does not disturb wheel speed.
